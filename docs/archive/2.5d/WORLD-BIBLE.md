# World & Environment Bible — Hybrid 2.5D

**Status:** proposal for review  
**World format:** illustrated sets arranged in layered 3D space

## 1. World philosophy

Locations should be instantly understandable, visually calm, and flexible enough to host many hypothetical stories. The environment is a stage for character acting and visual explanation—not a realistic world simulation.

## 2. Layer architecture

A typical shot may contain:
1. **Foreground:** framing objects, silhouettes, simple depth cues.
2. **Action plane:** character, hero props, contact shadows.
3. **Midground:** furniture, architecture, secondary props.
4. **Background:** large shapes, windows, skyline, gradients or illustrated scenery.
5. **Graphic overlay:** captions, labels, arrows, counters, diagrams.

Layers should be separated only when camera movement or animation benefits from it. Avoid unnecessary layer count.

## 3. Starter location families

### Everyday interior
- Simple room, office, bedroom, shop, classroom, or kitchen variants.
- Clear wall/floor separation, one or two large furniture forms, a small number of story props.
- Neutral base palette with one motivated accent.

### Urban exterior
- Sidewalk, street, building facades, storefront, park, or public square.
- Distinct foreground/midground/background silhouettes.
- Simplified perspective and limited signage.

### Abstract explanation space
- Clean graphic environment for concepts that do not need a literal location.
- Floating diagrams, scale comparisons, timelines, counters, and visual metaphors.
- Background kept quiet so the idea reads first.

## 4. Prop design

Props should have recognizable silhouettes and simplified detail. Build reusable families: phone, money, clock, chair, table, door, vehicle, food, tools, signs, and generic containers. Hero props may use simple 3D geometry when rotation or interaction adds value.

## 5. Palette and surface rules

- Use the project palette as a shared foundation.
- Keep large background regions lower contrast than the character.
- Use matte illustrated surfaces, controlled texture, and consistent outline treatment.
- Reserve strong saturation for the subject or story-critical prop.
- Avoid random procedural noise and unnecessary surface realism.

## 6. Depth and camera

- Organize assets around a consistent stage depth convention.
- Prefer orthographic or weak-perspective camera.
- Use parallax subtly; distant layers move less than near layers.
- Preserve contact shadows and visual grounding under characters and props.
- Avoid large camera rotations that expose the thin edges of 2D cutouts.

## 7. Continuity

Recurring locations preserve:
- Relative placement and scale of major shapes.
- Palette and outline style.
- Character interaction points.
- Prop positions when continuity matters.
- Layer depth and camera conventions.

A shot manifest should identify location version, visible layers, hero props, and any deliberate overrides.

## 8. Environment approval

Review each location from:
- Establishing wide view.
- Character medium view.
- Close interaction view.
- Reverse or alternate angle where needed.
- Actual camera move/parallax preview.

Reject floating props, unclear contact, distracting tangencies, unreadable perspective, and detail that competes with the action.
