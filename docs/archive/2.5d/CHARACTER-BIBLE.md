# Character Bible — Original 2.5D Protagonist

**Status:** concept proposal; not approved  
**Role:** recurring audience surrogate for hypothetical stories  
**Format:** illustrated 2D character with a reusable cutout rig

## 1. Design objective

Create a character recognizable from silhouette and color alone, expressive in a few frames, and practical to animate repeatedly. The character should feel like an original host—not a replica of any existing channel mascot.

## 2. Proposed identity: working concept

Working visual concept (name intentionally undecided):
- Warm ivory, softly angular face shape rather than a featureless circular head.
- Distinctive dark auburn side-swept hair with a small asymmetric forelock.
- Large but restrained dark eyes, clear eyebrows, and a simple mouth.
- Ochre short jacket over a deep-teal shirt.
- Small coral cross-body pouch as a recurring silhouette accent.
- Dark simple trousers and compact shoes.
- Slightly oversized head and hands for readable acting, with compact simplified body proportions.

This combination is a starting proposal. Final head shape, hair, clothing, proportions, and palette require review. Do not lock the design before a turnaround and expression sheet are approved.

## 3. Identity invariants

Keep stable across every shot:
- Head outline, hair silhouette, facial feature spacing.
- Jacket cut, shirt color, pouch shape and placement.
- Body-to-head proportions and limb lengths.
- Outline weight, fill colors, and shadow style.
- Hand and foot design.
- Rig pivots and neutral pose.

## 4. Required model sheets

Before production approval:
- Front, three-quarter, side, and back views.
- Neutral standing pose and silhouette-only view.
- Face close-up and expression sheet.
- Scale comparison with a door, chair, and handheld prop.
- Pose sheet: idle, walk, run, sit, point, reach, hold, react, celebrate, and explain.
- Hand shape sheet for pointing, open palm, fist, grip, and object interaction.

All views must depict the same design. Do not independently reinterpret the character for each angle.

## 5. Expression set

Minimum reusable expressions:
- Neutral / listening.
- Curious.
- Confused.
- Surprised.
- Worried.
- Excited.
- Frustrated.
- Determined.
- Relieved.
- Deadpan reaction.

Expressions must read at small sizes. Use eyebrow, eye direction, eyelid, and mouth changes before adding complex facial detail.

## 6. Cutout rig requirements

Proposed 2D rig layers:
- Head base, hair/back, hair/front.
- Eyes, brows, mouth shapes, optional eyelids.
- Neck and torso.
- Upper/lower arms and hands.
- Upper/lower legs and feet.
- Jacket/shirt/pouch overlay pieces where needed.

Rig should support head turns through designed angle swaps or limited 2.5D rotation—not distort a flat face through extreme perspective. Hands should be replaceable for clear prop interactions.

## 7. Performance library

Initial reusable actions:
- Idle/breathing (subtle).
- Talk/explain with hand gestures.
- Look left/right/up/down.
- Walk cycle and short run.
- Stop, turn, lean, shrug.
- Point, reach, pick up, hold, put down.
- Sit and stand.
- Quick surprise, confusion, concern, excitement, deadpan hold.

Every clip needs start/end poses, duration, intended emotion, and notes about whether it loops.

## 8. Approval gate

The character is not approved by one attractive image. Approval requires:
1. Turnaround.
2. Expression sheet.
3. Pose sheet.
4. Silhouette and thumbnail-size readability check.
5. Short acting test with at least three emotional beats.
6. Check against light, dark, warm, and cool backgrounds.
7. Confirmation that the design is distinct and does not depend on copied reference assets.
